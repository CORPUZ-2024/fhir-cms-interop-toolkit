# Policy Implications of MSSP PUF to FHIR R4 Gaps

The simultaneous emergence of CMS-0057-F interoperability mandates and the continued reliance on legacy flat-file formats like the MSSP PUF highlights a critical disconnect in the current health IT regulatory landscape. Our analysis reveals that core financial performance metrics, specifically savings rates and quality composite scores, have no direct equivalent in the FHIR R4 resource library. This structural gap exists not due to a lack of implementation rigor, but because the FHIR standard was primarily designed for clinical data exchange rather than population-level financial accountability. As TEFCA matures and FHIR becomes the dominant layer for cross-organizational exchange, value-based care programs will reach a bottleneck where financial performance data cannot flow through standard APIs without the introduction of entirely new resource types or extensive CMS-defined extension profiles.

Furthermore, the inherent patient-level architecture of FHIR presents a significant aggregation gap that complicates the transition to real-time analytics for Accountable Care Organizations. While resources like ExplanationOfBenefit exist to capture individual claims, the population-level insights found in the MSSP PUF—such as expenditure stratified by county or enrollment type—require a sophisticated preprocessing layer that is not native to the FHIR specification. Consequently, health IT platforms must independently develop custom aggregation engines to synthesize patient-level API responses into actionable organizational metrics. This ongoing need for manual data synthesis explains why flat-file datasets continue to serve as the primary source of truth for ACO performance evaluation even as Patient Access and Provider Access APIs proliferate.

Finally, the extension gap identified in coverage granularity underscores the limitations of current US Core profiles for specialized analytics. Standard US Core Coverage resources do not currently support the specific enrollment type codes, such as ESRD or Aged Dual status, that are vital for accurate risk adjustment and financial modeling. Payers attempting to comply with CMS-0057-F using off-the-shelf FHIR tooling will successfully expose basic coverage data but will fail to transmit the granular metadata necessary for advanced value-based care operations. Without the development of a unified CMS-specific implementation guide to standardize these extensions, the industry risks a fragmented data environment where the most critical analytical fields remain locked in non-standardized formats or proprietary API extensions.
